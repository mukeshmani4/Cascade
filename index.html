Design → Development → Accessibility

How's your design-to-development handoff working today?

If an accessibility miss only turns up in testing — or after launch — it's already cost a redesign, a rebuilt component, and time you don't get back before hitting the market. Cascade is built to catch it earlier, while it's still a decision on a screen.

Why this matters

The later a miss is caught, the more it costs to fix

A gap that starts in the design phase doesn't stay small. By the time it surfaces in testing or development, it's no longer a design tweak — it's a rebuild, a re-test, and a delay, all landing right before launch, when a team can least afford them.

What we're doing

Bring accessibility into the design conversation, not just the QA report

An overview of the shape of the solution — not a technical spec.

01

AI-assisted analysis

Looks at a design alongside accessibility standards and flags what's likely to cause problems for real users, before it ever reaches code.

02

Works from low-fidelity mockups

Doesn't wait for a polished design. Catches issues from the wireframe stage onward, while they're still cheap to change.

03

Suggestions, with human acceptance

Cascade proposes; a person decides. Nothing moves forward without someone on the team reviewing and accepting it first.

04

Fits the existing workflow

Design tool → Cascade → project management tool. A finding becomes tracked work without a manual bridge between systems.

05

Time spent early saves cost later

A few extra minutes considering accessibility during design is time that isn't spent re-testing, re-building, or firefighting close to launch.

About this page itself

A pitch about accessibility should be accessible

Not aspirational — implemented on this exact page, checked with both math and an automated audit before sharing it.

  • Skip linkThe first focusable element jumps straight to the main content.
  • Real landmarks, real heading orderHeader, nav, main and footer are actual HTML elements, with headings running h1 → h2 → h3 in order.
  • Text contrast checked, not eyeballedEvery text/background pairing was run through the WCAG contrast formula — all clear AA, most clear AAA.
  • Focus is always visibleTab through the page and you'll always see exactly where you are.
  • The cost chart isn't a screen-reader dead endIt's a real image with alt text describing the same trend shown visually.
  • Motion respects your settingsTransitions and smooth scrolling switch off if your system requests reduced motion.
  • Holds together at 320pxThe layout reflows for narrow screens without losing content.
